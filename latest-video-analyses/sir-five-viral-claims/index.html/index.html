SIR Fact Check: 5 Viral Claims About India’s Voter-List Revision

Special Intensive Revision (SIR) has become one of India’s biggest election controversies. These five claims mix draft exclusions, final deletions, voter eligibility, court rulings and allegations of political manipulation. Here is what the available records establish—and what they do not.

Truth Sab Tak Editorial Desk · October 7, 2026 · Sources reviewed through October 7, 2026
OVERALL VERDICT: CONTEXT REQUIRED

Large numbers of names were absent from draft rolls, and legal and procedural questions remain. Draft-stage figures alone do not prove that every affected person was eligible, permanently removed, or politically targeted.

Scope: This reviews five formulations in public debate. We did not receive or independently authenticate one specific viral post or video. Official figures, court rulings, reported positions and our conclusions are distinguished below.

1. “SIR permanently deleted 65 lakh voters in Bihar.”

Verdict: MISLEADING. ECI figures show around 65 lakh electors were not included in Bihar’s draft roll published August 1, 2025. The draft was not the final roll: claims and objections followed, and the final roll published September 30 contained about 7.42 crore electors.

The ECI summary lists 7.89 crore electors before SIR, 65 lakh absent from the draft, 3.66 lakh ineligible electors removed from the draft, and 21.53 lakh eligible electors added through Form 6. The final total was about 7.42 crore. These categories do not mean all 65 lakh were permanently removed or shared the same status.

The Supreme Court’s interim orders required publication of omitted names and reasons, and provided for assistance with objections. A draft-stage omission was not equivalent to final permanent exclusion. The Court later upheld the Bihar SIR framework, without making each individual entry immune from challenge.

Established: about 65 lakh names were missing from the draft. That number alone does not establish that all were eligible, permanently excluded, or wrongfully removed.

2. “SIR deleted more than 10 crore / 13 crore genuine voters.”

Verdict: UNPROVEN AS STATED. The Indian Express reported about 13.37 crore names absent from draft rolls across SIR phases, based on ECI/election-office figures as of September 1, 2026. This is a large draft-stage count; it does not establish that all were eligible voters unlawfully removed.

Roll revisions may remove entries reported as deceased, shifted, duplicate, untraceable or otherwise requiring verification. Administrative categories do not prove that every individual decision was right either. Each case requires evidence about eligibility, notice, response and final status.

“13 crore names absent from draft rolls” is materially different from “13 crore eligible voters were illegally disenfranchised.” The latter needs evidence of eligibility and wrongful exclusion beyond a before-and-draft count.

3. “The Supreme Court declared SIR unconstitutional.”

Verdict: FALSE FOR THE BIHAR SIR JUDGMENT. On May 27, 2026, the Supreme Court upheld the legal validity of Bihar’s SIR. It traced ECI authority to Article 324 of the Constitution read with Section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950, and found a legitimate purpose and proportionality with safeguards.

This ruling addressed the Bihar challenge. It does not make every implementation decision or deletion correct, or resolve every dispute about SIR in other states.

4. “The Supreme Court gave ECI unlimited power to decide who is an Indian citizen.”

Verdict: MISLEADING. The May 27 judgment recognized citizenship as relevant to electoral eligibility and permits a limited inquiry for electoral-roll purposes. It did not make ECI the final authority on citizenship under the Citizenship Act.

The Court distinguished electoral eligibility from a conclusive citizenship adjudication. Where formal determination is required, the matter must be referred to the competent authority under citizenship law. ECI’s inquiry is limited to electoral-roll requirements.

5. “The Supreme Court approved the ECI’s modified Form 6.”

Verdict: MISLEADING. The May 27 Bihar judgment should not be described as blanket approval of every later Form 6 version or added declaration. At an October 5, 2026 hearing, Chief Justice Surya Kant said the Supreme Court had not approved any modification to Form 6; reporting said the bench sought ECI records to examine who made changes.

ECI’s position was that an SIR-specific declaration was used during SIR, and the statutory form under the Registration of Electors Rules applies outside that phase. The distinction matters. Attribute the October 5 remarks to the reported oral clarification; do not present them as a final judgment on every issue.

What is established—and what remains unproven

  • Bihar’s draft omitted about 65 lakh names; the final roll later contained about 7.42 crore electors.
  • The Supreme Court upheld Bihar SIR’s legality and discussed safeguards and limits on citizenship inquiry.
  • Reported figures counted about 13.37 crore names absent from draft rolls across phases by September 1, 2026. This does not verify eligibility or permanent exclusion.
  • Transparency, remedies and implementation remain open to public and legal scrutiny. Aggregate figures alone do not prove partisan intent.

Truth Sab Tak — Bottom line

SIR is a large electoral-roll revision, and draft-stage exclusions are real. But deletion counts alone do not establish that every omitted person was a genuine voter illegally removed, or that the entire exercise was unconstitutional. The Supreme Court upheld the Bihar SIR framework; individual decisions and implementation remain open to scrutiny and challenge.
